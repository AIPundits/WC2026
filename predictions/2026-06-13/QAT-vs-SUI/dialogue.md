**Group B, Round 1 | 2026-06-13 19:00 UTC | Santa Clara**

**[TURN 1 — QAT PUNDIT]**

Switzerland come into this unbeaten through UEFA qualifying, with Granit Xhaka fully fit and Murat Yakin's system drilled to perfection. Our form going in is a serious concern: zero wins in five, one goal scored, six conceded. The 0-0 draw against El Salvador — a side that didn't even qualify — in our final warm-up told a story about our finishing problems that we cannot ignore.

But here's what I'll hold onto: we beat Switzerland 1-0 in the only previous meeting between these sides. That 2018 friendly win shows this group of players knows how to execute a tactical plan against European opposition. Akram Afif on the break is a genuine X-factor — his pace and directness can punish any defensive line that pushes too high. A low-block, disciplined defensive shape, and a single moment of Afif brilliance — that is Qatar's path to a result here.

The truth: I'm predicting a 0-2 defeat. I say that not with despair but with clarity — if we are going to have any chance of advancing from Group B, we must dramatically improve our clinical edge. Today may be a step too far.

**[TURN 1 — SUI PUNDIT]**

We hear Qatar's pundit, and we respect the honest self-assessment. But let's be clear about what Switzerland are bringing to Santa Clara.

We've had Granit Xhaka patrolling that midfield, setting tempo, shielding the back four — and he is fit, confirmed, and focused. Murat Yakin has had months to prepare a specific game plan for Qatar and that game plan is not complicated: control the centre, neutralise Afif's counter-attacking runs with our right-back tracking him closely, and exploit Qatar's defensive disorganisation at set pieces.

Qatar's form is genuinely alarming — not just the results but the *pattern*. One goal in five matches. A draw with El Salvador. These are not blips; they are structural. Qatar cannot score against teams who defend intelligently, and we intend to defend very intelligently indeed.

Our attacking threat comes from multiple angles: Embolo through the middle, movement off the flanks, and Xhaka arriving late from deep. We will not need to take risks. A composed, professional 2-0 win is what Switzerland are targeting, and on current evidence, we should deliver exactly that.

**[TURN 2 — QAT PUNDIT]**

Switzerland are right that our scoring record is the central problem. There's no point sugarcoating it. Akram Afif will need to be clinical when the one or two counter-attacking chances arrive — if he blazes them wide as Qatar have done all pre-tournament, then Swiss quality takes over.

What concerns me about our defensive shape is the pace of Switzerland's wide players. Their ability to switch flanks quickly can pin back our midfield and deny us the transition moments we need. If we can keep this 0-0 past the hour mark, however, the game changes — Switzerland would need to push forward and spaces open. That is the only scenario where Qatar can pinch something from this match.

My assessment stands: I expect a 0-2 defeat, but Afif's pace means Switzerland will never fully relax. I urge the players to make this uncomfortable for as long as possible.

We will maintain shape, we will keep probing, and Qatar's defenders will eventually commit an error under sustained pressure. Their lack of goals does not come from a lack of trying — it comes from poor technical execution in the final third, and that same technical frailty exists in their defensive third when pressed.

I am locking in 2-0 to Switzerland. Clean sheet is highly probable. Afif will have one moment — he always does — but Xhaka and the back four will manage it without a crisis.
