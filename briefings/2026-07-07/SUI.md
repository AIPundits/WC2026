## 4. Tournament Standing / Form

Switzerland enter their first-ever WC R16 win (since 1938) in strong form. The tactical blueprint under Murat Yakin is a settled 4-2-3-1 with Xhaka as the controlling deep midfielder and a dangerous counter-attacking threat through Manzambi's raw pace and Embolo's direct running. Freuler's energy across both phases provides the defensive shield. Advancing to the quarter-final would mark Switzerland's deepest-ever run at the World Cup — a milestone the squad appears genuinely capable of targeting. Xhaka has kept expectations managed publicly, but the quality of the R32 win made headlines globally.

## Calibration notes for pundits

- **Tight margins dominate knockout results** — of 18 decisive knockout results, 12 were settled by exactly 1 goal and only 6 by 2 or more. A 1-goal winning margin (e.g. 1-0, 2-1) is the knockout norm; predict a 2+ goal gap only when there is a clear quality and fitness gap evidenced by this tournament.
- **Knockout draw rate (level at 90 min): 14.3% (3/21 matches)** — a draw at 90 min triggers extra time. Do not set `draw_probability` below this rate without strong evidence of a decisive winner.
- **Clean sheets occur in 47.6% of knockout matches (10/21)** — one side failing to score is a genuine and common outcome. If a team has scored in fewer than half their tournament matches, or the opponent has conceded fewer than 1 goal per game, 0 goals for that team is the correct default — not 1.
- **Group stage scoring volume is evidence about individual teams, not knockout scorelines** — a team that scored consistently in the group stage has proven attacking quality that carries into the knockouts. An inability to score in the group stage is equally telling. But use the knockout frequency table above as your scoreline prior, not the group stage table.
- **Group stage draw rate: 27.8%** — relevant only for group stage fixtures.
- **Blowouts are rare in knockout rounds** — they occurred in the group stage (6-0, 7-1) but the knockout data shows far tighter margins. Only predict a large margin if the quality gap is exceptional and evidenced by this tournament, not just reputation.
