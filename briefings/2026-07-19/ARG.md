## 1. Tournament Journey

**Group Stage — Group J:**

- **Round 1 (Jun 16) vs Algeria: 3-0** — Messi announced his final World Cup with a hat-trick. The 38-year-old was irresistible in a dominant opening display. Scaloni's 4-4-2 with Messi and Álvarez up top worked to perfection; Algeria had no answer. Messi became the tournament's leading scorer from the first game.
- **Round 2 (Jun 22) vs Austria: 2-0** — Another clean sheet. Messi scored again (his fourth tournament goal after two games). Argentina's press was effective and Austria barely threatened Emiliano Martínez.
- **Round 3 (Jun 27) vs Jordan: 3-1** — Group J won. Argentina conceded for the first time but their firepower ensured a comfortable win. Messi ended the group stage with 6 goals.

**Knockout Stage:**

- **Round of 32 (Jul 03) vs Cape Verde: 3-2** — A harder test than expected. Cape Verde — unbeaten in the group stage after three draws — pushed Argentina hard, clawing back to 3-2 before the Albiceleste held on. A warning about defensive fragility.
- **Round of 16 (Jul 07) vs Egypt: 3-2** — High-scoring thriller. Argentina's attack was devastating but they conceded twice, underscoring that the defence remains exploitable.
- **Quarter-Final (Jul 12) vs Switzerland: 1-1 (AET)** — Argentina's toughest test. The game was level at 90 minutes and required extra time — Argentina won it in the added period. Their 13th World Cup match to go to extra time, breaking a record shared with Germany. Mental resilience on display. (Source: ESPN)
- **Semi-Final (Jul 15) vs England: 2-1** — Messi was extraordinary, completing **9 dribbles and providing 2 assists** — the first player on record (since 1966) to achieve both in a single WC knockout match. Lautaro Martínez scored the decisive goal. (Source: BBC Sport)

**Suspension Risk:** None. FIFA clears all accumulated yellow cards before the final. No player carries a suspension risk into Sunday. *Note: Argentina received attention after celebrating with a Falkland Islands banner post-match, but this is a team/federation matter and unlikely to produce individual player bans for the final.* (Source: AS USA)

## 4. Tournament Standing / Form

Argentina are the tournament's most prolific attacking force. **7 matches: W7 D0 L0 (1 required AET). Goals scored: 17 (2.43/game). Goals conceded: 7 (1.0/game). Clean sheets: 2/7.** The 2022 World Cup holders have been relentless in attack — no team in the tournament comes close to their goal tally. However, they have conceded in 5 of 7 games and show clear defensive vulnerability in transition. Messi (8 goals, 4 assists = 12 contributions) is the tournament's standout individual. A win on Sunday would make Argentina the first team since Brazil (1958/1962) to win back-to-back World Cups — and Messi's sixth would become the greatest individual achievement in football history.

### Calibration notes for pundits

- **Tight margins dominate knockout results** — of 25 decisive knockout results, 16 were settled by exactly 1 goal and only 9 by 2 or more. A 1-goal winning margin (e.g. 1-0, 2-1) is the knockout norm; predict a 2+ goal gap only when there is a clear quality and fitness gap evidenced by this tournament.
- **Knockout draw rate (level at 90 min): 16.7% (5/30 matches)** — a draw at 90 min triggers extra time. Do not set `draw_probability` below this rate without strong evidence of a decisive winner.
- **Clean sheets occur in 43.3% of knockout matches (13/30)** — one side failing to score is a genuine and common outcome. If a team has scored in fewer than half their tournament matches, or the opponent has conceded fewer than 1 goal per game, 0 goals for that team is the correct default — not 1.
- **Group stage scoring volume is evidence about individual teams, not knockout scorelines** — a team that scored consistently in the group stage has proven attacking quality that carries into the knockouts. An inability to score in the group stage is equally telling. But use the knockout frequency table above as your scoreline prior, not the group stage table.
- **Group stage draw rate: 27.8%** — relevant only for group stage fixtures.
- **Blowouts are rare in knockout rounds** — they occurred in the group stage (6-0, 7-1) but the knockout data shows far tighter margins. Only predict a large margin if the quality gap is exceptional and evidenced by this tournament, not just reputation.
