## 1. Tournament Journey

Argentina are the defending World Cup champions and have been the most dominant attacking team at this tournament — unbeaten in 6 matches (6W), scoring exactly 3 goals in every single game.

**Group J (perfect record):**
- **vs Algeria (Jun 16, W 3-0):** Dominant opening — Messi orchestrated, clean sheet, statement performance. Total control against an AFCON-calibre opposition.
- **vs Austria (Jun 22, W 2-0):** Efficient rather than spectacular. Argentina were structured and disciplined, limiting the counter-attack threat. Clean sheet.
- **vs Jordan (Jun 27, W 3-1):** Jordan's consolation goal masked the degree of control. Argentina rotated moderately, still automatic with the 4-1-2-3 system.

**Knockout stage:**
- **R32 vs Cape Verde (Jul 3, W 3-2 aet):** First test of character — Argentina were pushed to extra time but found the decisive goal. Showed resilience when their margin was cut. Messi-linked play crucial in aet.
- **R16 vs Egypt (Jul 7, W 3-2):** Egypt made it uncomfortable. Tagliafico's overlapping runs from left-back created penalty; **Messi missed the spot-kick** (second miss this tournament; 4th career WC penalty). Enzo Fernández threaded through-ball for Tagliafico to win the pen. Eventually won 3-2. Athletic: "Messi dropped deep, split the Egypt midfield to find Fernandez" — the deep-drop playmaker role fully evident.
- **QF vs Switzerland (Jul 11, W 3-1 aet):** Switzerland reduced to 10 men; **Julian Alvarez golazo** was the centrepiece. **Lautaro Martínez** scored in injury time of extra time to seal it. Argentina's third consecutive extra-time battle in the knockouts before this match was the first decided without extra time since R16. Source: FIFA.com, ESPN, Al Jazeera.

**Suspension Risk:** Yellow cards are wiped after the QF stage (confirmed by FIFA Article 10 para 2). **No accumulation risk for any Argentina player in this semi-final.** Red-card bans would still apply — none reported.

## 4. Tournament Standing / Form

Argentina are through to their third World Cup semi-final in four tournaments (2014 final, 2018 QF, 2022 champions, 2026 semi-final). In 6 matches: **6W 0D 0L, 17 goals scored (2.83/game), 8 conceded (1.33/game).** Two knockout games required extra time (R32, QF), but Argentina were not eliminated and did not concede more than 2 in any single match.

Scaloni's system centres on **Messi dropping deep** to link play: "In previous World Cups, Argentina had a plethora of attackers they tried to shoehorn into the side so Messi could provide for them. But in 2022 and now in 2026, they have a catalogue of players, such as Enzo Fernández and Alexis Mac Allister, who can progressively pass the ball through tight gaps." (ESPN) Tagliafico's overlapping runs from left-back are a specific weapon — he contributed to two chance sequences vs Egypt. **Messi has 8 tournament goals** (highest scorer), operating in short explosive actions rather than full-game press.

As defending champions, Argentina have knockout-stage experience and pedigree. They know how to win ugly (two aet victories) as well as dominantly.

## Calibration notes for pundits

- **Tight margins dominate knockout results** — of 23 decisive knockout results, 15 were settled by exactly 1 goal and only 8 by 2 or more. A 1-goal winning margin (e.g. 1-0, 2-1) is the knockout norm; predict a 2+ goal gap only when there is a clear quality and fitness gap evidenced by this tournament.
- **Knockout draw rate (level at 90 min): 14.8% (4/27 matches)** — a draw at 90 min triggers extra time. Do not set `draw_probability` below this rate without this rate without strong evidence of a decisive winner.
- **Clean sheets occur in 44.4% of knockout matches (12/27)** — one side failing to score is a genuine and common outcome. If a team has scored in fewer than half their tournament matches, or the opponent has conceded fewer than 1 goal per game, 0 goals for that team is the correct default — not 1.
- **Group stage scoring volume is evidence about individual teams, not knockout scorelines** — a team that scored consistently in the group stage has proven attacking quality that carries into the knockouts. An inability to score in the group stage is equally telling. But use the knockout frequency table above as your scoreline prior, not the group stage table.
- **Group stage draw rate: 27.8%** — relevant only for group stage fixtures.
- **Blowouts are rare in knockout rounds** — they occurred in the group stage (6-0, 7-1) but the knockout data shows far tighter margins. Only predict a large margin if the quality gap is exceptional and evidenced by this tournament, not just reputation.
