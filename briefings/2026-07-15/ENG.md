## 1. Tournament Journey

England have been the most consistent European team at this World Cup, navigating six matches unbeaten (5W 1D) across the group stage and three knockout rounds.

**Group L:**
- **vs Croatia (Jun 17, W 4-2):** Attacking firepower on show — Bellingham and Kane prominent, Rice/Saka set-piece delivery decisive. A nervy finish exposed defensive vulnerability at pace.
- **vs Ghana (Jun 23, D 0-0):** England's only drop of points. Failed to unlock a low block, no meaningful shots on target. Raised questions about creativity against compact defences.
- **vs Panama (Jun 27, W 2-0):** Professional group-stage closer; Tuchel briefly trialled a 4-1-4-1 but returned to the 4-2-3-1 for the knockouts.

**Knockout stage:**
- **R32 vs DR Congo (Jul 1, W 2-1):** Tight contest resolved by set-piece delivery. Rice/Saka outswinging corners were again decisive — a recurring tactical theme.
- **R16 vs Mexico (Jul 5, W 3-2):** England's most dramatic result. Their own prediction suggested a defeat; instead a 3-2 win. England exposed defensive vulnerabilities when Mexico pressed high. Fight-back quality proven.
- **QF vs Norway (Jul 11, W 2-1):** Bellingham's sixth tournament goal sealed progress. Reece James returned as a second-half substitute (49 minutes, no reported setback). The Athletic: England took more corners (24+) than any prior Southgate/Tuchel tournament — set pieces remain the primary delivery mechanism.

**Suspension Risk:** Yellow cards are wiped after the QF stage (confirmed by FIFA Article 10 para 2). **No accumulation risk for any England player in this semi-final.** Exception: **Jarell Quansah (CB)** serves a 2-match FIFA ban for serious foul play — OUT for this match and the final if applicable.

## 4. Tournament Standing / Form

England through to their second World Cup semi-final in three tournaments. In 6 matches: **5W 1D, 13 goals scored (2.17/game), 6 conceded (1.00/game)**. They have scored in every match, but **conceded in 4 of 6 matches** — a pattern that indicates Argentina's attack will find chances.

Set pieces are England's most reliable weapon: corners and free-kick delivery from Rice and Saka have produced multiple goals. Bellingham (6 tournament goals) is the most dangerous runner from deep. Advancing to the final would be England's first World Cup final appearance since 1966 — their only title.

## Calibration notes for pundits

- **Tight margins dominate knockout results** — of 23 decisive knockout results, 15 were settled by exactly 1 goal and only 8 by 2 or more. A 1-goal winning margin (e.g. 1-0, 2-1) is the knockout norm; predict a 2+ goal gap only when there is a clear quality and fitness gap evidenced by this tournament.
- **Knockout draw rate (level at 90 min): 14.8% (4/27 matches)** — a draw at 90 min triggers extra time. Do not set `draw_probability` below this rate without strong evidence of a decisive winner.
- **Clean sheets occur in 44.4% of knockout matches (12/27)** — one side failing to score is a genuine and common outcome. If a team has scored in fewer than half their tournament matches, or the opponent has conceded fewer than 1 goal per game, 0 goals for that team is the correct default — not 1.
- **Group stage scoring volume is evidence about individual teams, not knockout scorelines** — a team that scored consistently in the group stage has proven attacking quality that carries into the knockouts. An inability to score in the group stage is equally telling. But use the knockout frequency table above as your scoreline prior, not the group stage table.
- **Group stage draw rate: 27.8%** — relevant only for group stage fixtures.
- **Blowouts are rare in knockout rounds** — they occurred in the group stage (6-0, 7-1) but the knockout data shows far tighter margins. Only predict a large margin if the quality gap is exceptional and evidenced by this tournament, not just reputation.
