## 1. Tournament Journey

Canada entered the 2026 World Cup in **Group B** alongside Switzerland, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Qatar. They qualified for the knockout stage as group runners-up — their first-ever progression to a World Cup knockout round.

**Group Stage Results:**

- **Jun 12 vs Bosnia and Herzegovina (D 1-1)**: An opening draw in front of a home crowd at Toronto Stadium. Frustrating against a side Canada were expected to beat, but a point on the board. Alphonso Davies was absent (hamstring), his absence used by Marsch as a tactical "decoy" to keep opponents guessing.
- **Jun 18 vs Qatar (W 6-0)**: **Canada's first-ever World Cup victory** — a historic rout. Jonathan David and Cyle Larin led the attack. However, the match was overshadowed: Ismael Koné suffered a broken leg from a dangerous challenge by Qatar's Assim Madibo (banned 5 matches by FIFA DisCo) and is out for the rest of the tournament.
- **Jun 24 vs Switzerland (L 1-2)**: A defeat that confirmed Canada in second place. Switzerland's quality told; Canada created chances but conceded twice and could only reply with one goal. Stephen Eustaquio battled through a knock (reported "back from his own knock" per RotoWire ahead of the R32).

**Suspension Risk:**
- Under 2026 World Cup rules, **single yellow cards are fully reset after the group stage**. Canada enter the Round of 32 with all players on a clean yellow card slate.
- Any player booked today starts the Round of 16 on one yellow. A second yellow in R16 would trigger a quarter-final ban.
- No Canada players carried a two-yellow ban into the final group game.

## 4. Tournament Form Summary

**3 WC matches: 8 goals scored (2.7/game), 3 conceded (1.0/game). Record: 1W 1D 1L.**

Canada's tournament has been defined by extremes. The 6-0 vs Qatar (Canada's greatest-ever WC result) proved their attacking potential is elite when clicking. But draws and defeats against Bosnia and Switzerland showed inconsistency. Conceding 3 goals in 3 games is acceptable; only scoring in patches outside the Qatar mauling is the worry.

The return of Alphonso Davies is the story of this match. He is Canada's most recognisable and dangerous player — a two-time Champions League winner at Bayern Munich. His absence fundamentally limited the team's left-side threat in the group stage.

This is Canada's **first-ever knockout match** in a men's World Cup — a historic moment for Canadian football. Jesse Marsch and the squad will approach it with immense motivation.

## Calibration notes for pundits

- **1-0 is the most common result** — do not require 2+ goals from the stronger team unless there is clear attacking evidence.
- **2-1 is historically over-predicted** — it accounts for a small fraction of actual results; favour 1-0 or 2-0 for one-sided contests.
- **Draws occur in roughly 27.8% of group stage matches** — if your draw probability is well below this, you are likely under-pricing level results.
- **Blowouts happen** — scorelines of 4-0, 5-1, 6-0 have occurred. When one team is structurally outclassed, wider margins are more likely than the 2-1 default.
