## 1. Tournament Journey

Austria finished **2nd in Group J** (Argentina, Algeria, Jordan) with 4 points, a result that felt hard-earned against a brutally difficult group. They opened with a confident **3-1 victory over Jordan** (June 16), showcasing Rangnick's high-energy pressing style. A disciplined but ultimately **0-2 defeat to Argentina** (June 22) followed — controlled damage against the group's heavyweights. The group concluded with a breathless **3-3 draw with Algeria** (June 27) in Kansas City, featuring two Austrian stoppage-time goals in a match that sent both teams through. Sasa Kalajdzic was among the standout contributors off the bench.

The journey revealed both the promise of Rangnick's system — genuine pressing intensity, a willingness to attack — and its defensive fragility (6 goals conceded). Against Argentina they were cautious and limited; against Algeria they were electric and chaotic. Spain will present an entirely different tactical challenge.

**Suspension Risk:** All group stage yellow cards wiped at end of group phase — no Austria players carry a booking into the Round of 32. Clean slate.

## 4. Tournament Standing / Form

Austria qualified from Group J as runners-up with 4 points — an outcome that required dramatic stoppage-time goals to secure. The attacking stats are genuinely impressive (6 goals in 3 group games) but the defensive record (6 conceded) is a serious concern when facing Spain's clinical attack, even in its injury-weakened state. Rangnick's system thrives on transition and intensity — sustaining that against Rodri and Pedri across 90+ minutes will be the central challenge. This is **Austria's first WC knockout match since the 1954 tournament**, when they finished third. The occasion itself is historic.

Advancing from the R32 would set up a Round of 16 meeting with Portugal or Croatia.
