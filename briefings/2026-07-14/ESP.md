## 1. Tournament Journey

**Group Stage (Group H):**
Spain's tournament opened with a shock 0-0 draw against Cabo Verde in Atlanta — a result that raised early questions about De la Fuente's side. They responded emphatically: a 4-0 dismantling of Saudi Arabia and then a hard-fought 1-0 win over Uruguay (Guadalajara) sealed group progression. Group stage total: 5 goals scored, 0 conceded (excluding the 0-0).

**Knockout Stage:**
Spain have been ruthlessly clinical since the R32. A 3-0 win over Austria (R32) was followed by a tight but controlled 1-0 defeat of Portugal in Dallas (R16) — a Iberian derby settled by the narrowest margin. The Quarter-Final against Belgium was their most demanding test: a 2-1 win that showed Spain can absorb pressure and still produce in high-intensity knockout football. Only **1 goal conceded** across all 6 tournament matches.

Luis de la Fuente's vertical 4-3-3 has been a model of balance: Rodri commanding and protecting, Yamal and Williams terrorising in wide areas, and Oyarzabal providing the clinical edge in attack. Spain are defending champions (Euro 2024) playing with the swagger and tactical sophistication of a team at their peak.

**Suspension Risk:**
⚠️ **Ferran Torres** (FW) was shown a yellow card in the R16 against Portugal. Reports ahead of the QF vs Belgium flagged him as one booking away from missing the semi-final if booked in that match. It is unclear whether yellows were wiped after the QF under 2026 FIFA rules — if not, Torres may carry a yellow into this match. **Monitor his availability and booking risk.** His name does not appear in Sports Mole's Spain suspension list for the semi-final (which focuses on injuries to Pino, Williams, Munoz), suggesting he may be clear — but verify before the match. *(Sources: Squawka, CryptoBriefing, Sports Mole)*

No other Spain players are reported to be on a yellow card suspension risk for the semi-final.

## 4. Tournament Standing / Form

Spain enter the semi-final with arguably the **best defensive record in the tournament**:
- **6 played | 5 won | 1 drawn | 0 lost**
- **11 goals scored (1.83/game) | 1 conceded (0.17/game)**
- Knockout record: 3W, 6 scored, 1 conceded (the Belgium QF)

Only conceding 1 goal in 6 World Cup matches is extraordinary. Advancing to the final would make Spain the first team since Brazil (2002) to reach the WC final having conceded fewer than 2 goals in the entire tournament. Spain's psychological strength as defending European champions — and the fact they eliminated France in the Euro 2024 semi-final — makes them a genuine co-favourite entering this match. De la Fuente's side are a known quantity: they want to control possession, stretch opponents wide, and manufacture scoring opportunities through patient combination play.

## Calibration notes for pundits

- **Tight margins dominate knockout results** — of 23 decisive knockout results, 15 were settled by exactly 1 goal and only 8 by 2 or more. A 1-goal winning margin (e.g. 1-0, 2-1) is the knockout norm; predict a 2+ goal gap only when there is a clear quality and fitness gap evidenced by this tournament.
- **Knockout draw rate (level at 90 min): 14.8% (4/27 matches)** — a draw at 90 min triggers extra time. Do not set `draw_probability` below this rate without strong evidence of a decisive winner.
- **Clean sheets occur in 44.4% of knockout matches (12/27)** — one side failing to score is a genuine and common outcome. If a team has scored in fewer than half their tournament matches, or the opponent has conceded fewer than 1 goal per game, 0 goals for that team is the correct default — not 1.
- **Group stage scoring volume is evidence about individual teams, not knockout scorelines** — a team that scored consistently in the group stage has proven attacking quality that carries into the knockouts. An inability to score in the group stage is equally telling. But use the knockout frequency table above as your scoreline prior, not the group stage table.
- **Group stage draw rate: 27.8%** — relevant only for group stage fixtures.
- **Blowouts are rare in knockout rounds** — they occurred in the group stage (6-0, 7-1) but the knockout data shows far tighter margins. Only predict a large margin if the quality gap is exceptional and evidenced by this tournament, not just reputation.
